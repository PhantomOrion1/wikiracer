How to play

A wiki race (also called the Wikipedia game or a Wikipedia speedrun) is simple: you start on one Wikipedia article and race to another using only the links inside the articles. Fewest clicks wins.

Start a racePlay with friends

The rules

  1. You land on the start article. The target is shown at the top of the screen the whole time.
  2. Click links inside the article. No search box, no typing a URL, no external sites. Links to categories, files and other wikis are disabled.
  3. Reach the target. The moment its article opens, you're done. The lowest number of clicks wins, and ties go to the faster time.

What counts as a click

Every article you open counts as one click, including going back with the Go Back button. Jumping to a section inside the same article is free.

Time limits

DifficultyTimeWhat the pair is like
Easy2:30Well-known topics that are about two clicks apart.
Medium5:00Topics from different areas with no direct link between them.
Hard10:00No two-click route exists. You'll need a plan.

Fair play

  • Leaving the tab gets you one warning, then a +1 click penalty each time.
  • Find in page (Ctrl/⌘+F) doesn't work on links, so you have to actually read.
  • Every multiplayer route is checked against Wikipedia's real links, and finishes that are impossibly fast get flagged.

Tips from fast racers

  • Go broad, then narrow. Countries, cities, centuries and big subjects ("United States", "World War II", "Biology") link to almost everything.
  • Think backwards. Ask what article would definitely link to the target, then aim for that.
  • Use the first paragraph and the infobox. The most useful links are usually near the top.
  • Don't over-read. In a race, scanning beats reading. Back-tracking costs a click, so commit once you spot a good hub.

Game modes

Quick match drops you into a public room with other players. Private rooms let you race up to 8 friends with a room code (how it works). Solo lets you practise at any difficulty, and the Daily challenge gives everyone the same hard pair each day (past dailies).
