Wikipedia speedrun

A Wikipedia speedrun is a race from one Wikipedia article to another using only the links inside the pages. It's the same game as a wiki race. "Speedrun" just puts the focus on beating your own best.

Two ways to score a run

FormatWinnerGood for
Fewest clicksShortest route; time breaks tiesThinking, planning, fair on slow connections
Fastest timeFirst to arrive, however many clicksStreams, parties, quick reflexes

WikiRacer Live uses fewest clicks, then time, so a careful route beats frantic clicking, but speed still matters when two players tie.

Why almost every run is short

English Wikipedia has millions of articles, but they're densely linked. Most pairs of articles are only three or four clicks apart, because a small number of "hub" articles (big countries, major cities, centuries, broad sciences) link to a huge share of everything else. A good run is about finding the right hub quickly.

Strategies fast runners use

1. Climb, then descend

From a narrow start, click up to something broad (a country, a field, a time period), then come back down towards the target's world. "Croissant → France → Europe → …" climbs fast.

2. Work backwards from the target

Before clicking anything, ask what page would definitely link to the target. For "Volcano" that's "Plate tectonics". For a footballer, it's their club or country. Now you're racing to an easier target.

3. Read the top, not the whole page

The lead paragraph and the infobox hold the densest, most general links. Long articles hide good links far down, but scrolling costs seconds.

4. Know your hubs

"United States", "United Kingdom", "World War II", "Latin", "Biology", "Mathematics" and the century pages ("20th century") connect almost everything. Learn which ones lead where.

5. Don't back-track unless you're sure

Going back costs a click. A slightly worse link forward is usually better than a step backwards.

Practise

  • Daily challenge: one hard pair a day, the same for everyone. Compare your route with past days in the daily archive.
  • Solo: Easy, Medium and Hard with time limits of 2:30, 5:00 and 10:00.
  • Race ideas: 50 pairs sorted by difficulty on the ideas page.
  • Race people: Quick match or a private room shows everyone's full route at the end, which is the fastest way to learn new shortcuts.
