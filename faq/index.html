FAQ

Quick answers about wiki races and how WikiRacer Live works.

What is a wiki race?

A wiki race is a game where players start on one Wikipedia article and try to reach a target article by clicking only the links inside the pages. The player who gets there in the fewest clicks wins, and ties go to the faster time. It’s also called the Wikipedia game, Wiki Game or Wikipedia speedrun.

Is WikiRacer Live free?

Yes. It’s free to play, there are no ads, and you don’t need an account.

How many people can play together?

Up to 8 players per room. Create a private room and share the 5-letter code or invite link, or use Quick match to join a public room.

Can I choose the start and target articles?

Yes. In a private room or in Solo, pick Custom and type any two English Wikipedia articles. You can also share a challenge link so friends race the same pair.

What counts as a click?

Every article you open counts as one click, including using Go Back. Jumping to a section within the same article is free.

Can I use Ctrl+F or the search bar?

No. Search isn’t available, and Find in page doesn’t match link text, so you have to read and scan the article yourself.

What happens if I switch tabs?

The article is hidden until you come back, so you can’t keep it open next to a search. The clock keeps running while you’re away.

How do you stop cheating?

Every multiplayer route is re-checked against Wikipedia’s actual links, and finishes that are faster than anyone could read the pages are flagged in the results.

What is the daily challenge?

Each day everyone gets the same hard pair. You get one attempt, ranked by clicks and then time.

Does it work on phones?

Yes. It works in modern mobile browsers, including Safari on iPhone and Chrome on Android.

Is this made by Wikipedia?

No. WikiRacer Live is an independent fan project. It isn’t affiliated with or endorsed by the Wikimedia Foundation. Article text comes from Wikipedia under the CC BY-SA 4.0 licence.

Is WikiRacer Live suitable for classrooms?

Many teachers use wiki races to practise reading, research and reasoning about how topics connect. Private rooms keep a class together, and you can set the pair yourself. Article content comes straight from Wikipedia, so supervise younger students.
